02Bench notes

  1. After a refoam

    Run the bass sweep at a modest level with the driver face up on the bench. Rub from an off-center coil, or a surround glued slightly crooked, shows up as a scratchy edge riding on the tone. When you hear it, stop the sweep and park on that frequency with the slider or the −/+ buttons.

  2. Chasing rattles

    Door cards, license plates, trim panels, the sub box itself. Sweep 30 – 100 Hz with the system turned up, note where the buzz starts, then hold that frequency and press on panels until it stops.

  3. Checking each driver

    Walk a sine from 50 Hz up past 10 kHz at low volume and listen near each driver in turn. A dead tweeter goes quiet at the top; a damaged woofer usually sounds rough or distorted at the bottom, well before it should.

  4. Two signals at once PRO

    Two tones a few hertz apart beat audibly, which makes small differences easy to hear. Set both to the same frequency and invert one, and they cancel. Pulse mode gives you a repeating tone burst instead of a steady signal.

  5. Taking a signal with you PRO

    Record a tone to a WAV file and play it from anything with a USB port or an aux input: a head unit, a DAW, the mixer at a gig.

LEVEL

Turn it down first. A steady tone puts continuous power into a voice coil in a way music never does, and tweeters are the first to cook. Square and sawtooth waves carry strong harmonics well above the fundamental: a 2 kHz square wave also puts real energy into the tweeter at 6 and 10 kHz.

A phone's own speaker gives out below a few hundred hertz, so a 40 Hz tone through it sounds like nothing. Use a cable into the system you're testing, or Bluetooth if that's what you've got.
